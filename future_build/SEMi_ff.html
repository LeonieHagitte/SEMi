Do students with higher socioeconomic status report higher academic motivation?

  • Socioeconomic status is continuous.
  • Measurement invariance is typically evaluated using groups.
  • Researchers therefore create categories such as low, medium, and high SES.
  • But these categories are arbitrary.

Can we evaluate measurement invariance without categorizing SES?

Why this is a measurement problem

Latent variables are not observed directly.

They are inferred from item responses.

\[ x_{ij} = \nu_j + \lambda_j \eta_i + \varepsilon_{ij} \]

  • \(\eta_i\) is the latent trait of person \(i\).
  • \(\lambda_j\) links item \(j\) to the latent trait.
  • \(\nu_j\) captures the expected item response when \(\eta_i = 0\).
  • Meaningful comparisons require that these measurement parameters are stable.

Before comparing latent motivation across SES, we must ask whether the items measure motivation equivalently.
